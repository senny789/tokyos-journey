# Tokyo sprite direction

Generated with the built-in image generator, without Phaser's sprite service.

Tokyo is a female golden retriever, she/her. Natural four-legged dog, warm honey-gold coat, cream muzzle and chest, floppy ears, black nose and dark eyes, fluffy tail. No clothing or accessories. Crisp SNES-era 16-bit pixel art with a dark brown outline and transparent background.

Production prompt: Create a sprite sheet with exactly eight equal columns and six equal rows. Keep Tokyo side-on and facing right, with the same identity, scale and proportions throughout. Row 1 is an eight-frame idle loop. Row 2 is an eight-frame walk. Row 3 is an eight-frame galloping run. Row 4 is takeoff, rising, apex, falling and landing poses. Row 5 is a low crouch loop. Row 6 is sitting down, seated idle and standing up. Use consistent foot baselines, generous clear margins and no text, grid, scenery or watermark.

`tokyo-source.png` preserves the generated original. `scripts/prepare-sprites.ps1` slices and samples that sheet into the runtime atlas at 64 pixels per frame with a shared foot baseline and binary alpha. The atlas is `game/assets/tokyo.png`.

Exact chunky warm 16-bit pixel art, same dark warm outline width, flat honey golden coat, light cream muzzle and chest, floppy dark-honey ear, black nose and dark eye, same paw size and long feathery cream-tipped tail as supplied. Keep the eye, ears and muzzle a good readable size. All four natural dog legs, no clothes or accessories. No text, no labels, no shadows, no floor or props. Keep the whole tail and both ends of the figure visible with padding.

- **Lowering**. Precisely SIX evenly spaced frames of a single transition from just below upright into a low belly crawl. With constant scale, same horizontal position, and stable planted paws, show successive phases lowering chest and bending front elbows, folding hind hocks with body centered, gradually lowering the hips and muzzle until the belly and chest hover just above the ground. Frame 1 looks like the accepted standing silhouette with knees just flexed. Frame 6 is nearly a natural low crawl, with paws reaching forward and tail trailing low. Her ears hang from the same point on the head and do not grow or shrink. Her eyes look in the direction she is facing. Keep the same grounded quadruped proportions and foot spacing.
- **Crawling**. Precisely EIGHT evenly spaced frames of a slow belly crawl cycle. Give her a low overall silhouette through deliberate anatomy and the tucked elbows and hocks. Keep her head and eyes proportional to the reference and recognize the same retriever. Each cycle phase differs by small alternating short reaches and planted-paw pushes, with a smooth progression so phase eight leads naturally back to one. Her far paws are slightly darker than near paws. Stay close to the same body length across all frames. She crawls on bent legs; no dragging her body without stepping. Neutral focused relaxed face, mostly closed mouth.
- **Eating**. Precisely SIX evenly spaced stages of a natural standing golden retriever lowering her head to eat from a low invisible bowl. Paws remain planted in the same position on all FOUR legs and legs stay upright supporting her at the same scale, hindquarters remain at standing height throughout. Frame 1 just starts to angle her nose down from the exact standing pose. Frame 2 the neck reaches forward and down halfway. Frame 3 her cream muzzle is just above the ground in the eating position. Frame 4 a tiny nibble with a small jaw movement and a subtle head bob at the same low height. Frame 5 her muzzle has lifted slightly while chewing. ## Taller crawl revision

The active lowering and crawl rows use the walking and sitting sprites as the reference. Built-in ImageGen drew the new poses, Sprite Gen extracted the individual figures, and Sprite Cast PixelKit packed them without compressing their height. The crawl has a 21–22 pixel silhouette, a y47 foot baseline, and a 41-unit collision body for the 44-unit gaps in the Playground and Movement Meadow.

> Production pixel-art animation for Tokyo’s Journey. Reference image shows the same accepted golden retriever walking and sitting: use its adult head size, light cream muzzle, large floppy ear, eyes and dark nose, honey coat, full fluffy cream-tipped tail, articulated paws, and warm chocolate outline EXACTLY. Right-facing strict side view. Draw one SINGLE very wide horizontal row of independent complete poses, genuinely transparent RGBA, clear transparent space between poses and generous margins. Same body length, head size, bone lengths and art pixel scale in every pose as the full-sized reference dog. Paws contact one fixed ground baseline. Readable drawn square pixel clusters, no blur or semi-transparent outline. No ground, shadows, text, clothing, props, or other objects. The overall torso stays full and round. For the low poses aim for a silhouette about one-half as tall as it is long (INCLUDING the tail). The crouched dog should retain about two-thirds of her standing height as measured from the ground to the top of the head; she never shrinks, flattens, lies on her stomach, or drags her legs.

- **Lowering**. Draw precisely SIX phases of slowly lowering the same adult dog from her upright walking reference into a crouched walk stance. Start close to the upright height, then naturally bend the knees, hocks and front elbows in gradual increments, lower the chest and hips, tuck the head slightly toward the shoulders, and finish at roughly two-thirds of standing height, with the head held at shoulder level. The last dog has four visibly bent weight-bearing legs and its chest and belly hover over the floor. Keep its head, muzzle, ear, torso and tail volume exactly the same in every frame. All feet stay near their standing-contact locations, with no lateral travel. Natural balanced lowering, rather than a reclining or lying-down pose.
- **Crawling**. Draw precisely EIGHT phases of a natural low crouched walk cycle. Tokyo ducks her head slightly in line with her shoulders, lowers her hips by bending rear hocks, folds front elbows low, and keeps her chest and belly raised a little above the ground. Keep her head the same size as the walking reference, with full muzzle, visible eye, and floppy ear. Keep the tail long and softly trailing low, not tucked under. Show clear, DIFFERENT grounded paw contacts across eight images: near forepaw reaches forward, plants, bears weight while the far rear paw advances, then the far front paw reaches and bears weight while the near rear paw steps. Use subtle weight transfer but keep the silhouette size and eye height consistent. Eight sequential low walking phases with discernible bent foreleg and hind-leg steps. She looks relaxed and alert, mouth gently closed or slightly open.
